Missing teeth: replacement options, planning & what to expect.

Missing one or more teeth can affect chewing, speech or confidence. Replacement is not one-size-fits-all; a dentist can compare options based on your mouth, health, remaining teeth and goals.

Patient educationThis guide explains common dental concerns in general terms. It is not a diagnosis and does not replace an examination by a qualified dental professional.

What happens when a tooth is missing?

A missing tooth can change how you chew and may affect the way nearby teeth contact one another. The practical impact depends on which tooth is missing, how long it has been missing and the health of the surrounding teeth and gums.

What are the replacement options?

Common options include a bridge, a removable partial denture or a dental implant supporting a crown. Some people may not need immediate replacement, while others benefit from replacing the tooth for function or other reasons. The choice depends on the individual case.

What to noticeChanges in pain, swelling, bleeding, sensitivity, function or appearance.
What an assessment addsClinical examination helps separate similar-looking symptoms and identify the right next step.

What can a dentist do?

The dentist assesses the missing area, neighbouring teeth, gums, bite and available bone. Imaging may be recommended when appropriate. They can then explain the benefits, limitations, maintenance and sequence of each suitable option before treatment begins.

How do dental implants work?

An implant is placed into the jawbone to provide support for a crown, bridge or denture. Implant treatment usually involves assessment, planning, placement, healing and restoration. Not every patient or site is suitable, so clinical evaluation is essential.

What can you do meanwhile?

Keep the remaining teeth and gums clean, attend regular examinations and avoid using a damaged temporary appliance without professional advice. If a missing tooth followed an injury or extraction, tell your dentist about the circumstances and timing.

Can future tooth loss be reduced?

Preventive care focuses on controlling tooth decay and gum disease, protecting teeth from injury, avoiding tobacco and attending dental reviews. Replacing a missing tooth does not remove the need to care for the remaining teeth.

Questions patients often ask

Can a missing tooth be left alone?

Sometimes a dentist may recommend monitoring rather than replacement, but the decision depends on the tooth, bite, function and oral health.

Are implants suitable for everyone?

No.

What is the alternative to an implant?

Bridges and removable dentures can also replace missing teeth.
