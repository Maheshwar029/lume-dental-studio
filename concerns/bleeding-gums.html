Bleeding gums: why it happens and how to respond.

Bleeding when you brush or clean between your teeth is common, but it should not simply be ignored. It can be a sign of gum inflammation and, in some people, more advanced periodontal disease.

What do bleeding gums mean?

Healthy gums generally do not bleed regularly during routine brushing. Bleeding, redness, swelling, tenderness or persistent bad breath can indicate inflammation caused by plaque. More advanced gum disease can affect the tissues and bone supporting teeth.

Why might gums bleed?

The most common reason is plaque build-up around the gumline and between teeth. Other factors can contribute, including inconsistent oral hygiene, smoking, some medicines, hormonal changes and conditions that affect health. A dentist can assess the pattern rather than assuming every case has the same cause.

Possible sourceDental decay, gum inflammation, sensitivity, damage or infection.
Why assessment mattersDifferent causes can feel similar but need different treatment.

What can a dentist do?

Your dentist can examine the gums, measure areas around teeth when appropriate and look for plaque, calculus, gum recession or signs of periodontal damage. Early gum disease is often managed with improved daily plaque control and professional cleaning. More established disease may require deeper professional cleaning and, in selected cases, periodontal treatment or referral.

How can you care for your gums at home?

Brush twice daily with fluoride toothpaste and clean between your teeth every day with floss or appropriately sized interdental brushes. Gentle, consistent cleaning is important; stopping because gums bleed can allow plaque to remain. Avoid smoking and follow the personalised cleaning advice given by your dental team.

When should you seek dental care quickly?

Arrange a dental assessment if bleeding is persistent, gums are very sore or swollen, teeth feel loose, there are unexplained mouth ulcers or patches, or symptoms are getting worse. Significant swelling, severe pain or other signs of an acute infection require prompt assessment.

Seek urgent help

If swelling around the mouth, face or neck makes it difficult to breathe, swallow or speak, seek emergency medical care immediately.

Can gum disease be prevented?

Good daily plaque control, regular dental reviews, professional cleaning when recommended, not smoking and managing relevant health conditions can reduce risk. Your recall interval should be based on your individual oral health rather than a fixed schedule for everyone.

Questions patients often ask

Should I stop brushing if my gums bleed?

Usually, stopping routine cleaning allows plaque to remain.

Can bleeding gums be cured?

Early gum inflammation can often improve substantially when plaque is controlled and contributing factors are addressed.

Is mouthwash enough?

Mouthwash does not replace brushing and cleaning between teeth.

Where to go next

If your symptoms are new, persistent or worrying you, an assessment is the safest way to understand the cause.
