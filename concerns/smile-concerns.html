Smile concerns: understanding cosmetic dental options.

A smile concern may involve colour, shape, spacing, worn teeth, missing teeth or the way teeth show when you smile. Cosmetic dentistry starts with understanding the concern and checking oral health first.

Patient educationThis guide explains common dental concerns in general terms. It is not a diagnosis and does not replace an examination by a qualified dental professional.

What counts as a smile concern?

Patients may want to discuss tooth colour, staining, uneven edges, spaces, proportions, worn or damaged teeth, or the appearance of restorations. A concern can be purely cosmetic, or it can overlap with tooth health, function or bite.

Why does a dentist assess oral health first?

Cosmetic treatment works best when teeth and gums are healthy enough to support the planned result. The dentist may assess decay, gum health, bite, existing fillings or crowns and tooth structure before discussing cosmetic options.

What to noticeChanges in pain, swelling, bleeding, sensitivity, function or appearance.
What an assessment addsClinical examination helps separate similar-looking symptoms and identify the right next step.

What can a dentist do?

Depending on the concern, options may include professional whitening, bonding, veneers, orthodontic treatment or restorative care. The right combination depends on the condition of the teeth, the desired change and what can be achieved predictably.

How is a smile plan created?

The process starts with your priorities. The dentist reviews your smile, discusses realistic outcomes and explains alternatives, limitations, maintenance and the expected sequence before you decide whether to proceed.

How can you protect a cosmetic result?

Good brushing and interdental cleaning, regular dental reviews, sensible dietary habits and avoiding habits that damage teeth help protect both natural teeth and restorations. Maintenance needs vary by treatment.

What does a natural result mean?

A natural-looking result considers tooth shape, proportion, colour, facial features and function rather than applying the same design to every patient. Your anatomy and the condition of your teeth place limits on what is appropriate.

Questions patients often ask

Is cosmetic dentistry only about appearance?

Not always.

Can whitening fix every discolouration?

No.

Do I need veneers for a small cosmetic concern?

Not necessarily.
