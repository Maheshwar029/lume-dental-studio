Common urgent dental concerns.

These examples are general guidance, not a diagnosis. A dentist should assess your situation.

01

Severe tooth pain

Persistent or severe pain can be associated with inflammation, infection or damage and may need prompt dental assessment.

02

Swelling or infection

Painful swelling around a tooth or gum can require urgent evaluation, particularly when it is worsening.

03

Broken tooth

A fractured tooth causing pain or soft-tissue trauma should be assessed so the dentist can determine appropriate care.

04

Knocked-out tooth

Dental trauma can require urgent assessment. Contact the clinic promptly for instructions and available care.

05

Bleeding

Bleeding that does not stop needs urgent attention. Contact the clinic for guidance.

06

Post-treatment problems

Severe pain, swelling or other concerning symptoms after dental treatment should be reported to the dental team.

First, we understand what is happening.

The care team can ask about your symptoms, timing and medical context, then determine whether you need an urgent dental appointment or a different level of care.

Know when a dental problem is also a medical emergency.

If you have difficulty breathing, uncontrolled bleeding, or swelling that may affect your airwaySeek emergency medical care immediately. Do not wait for a routine dental appointment.
